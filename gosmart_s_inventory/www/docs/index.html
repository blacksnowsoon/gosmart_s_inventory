Go Smart Simple Inventory Management System

A comprehensive guide to managing your inventory effectively.

Getting Started

Welcome to Go Smart Simple Inventory Management System! This guide will help you understand and effectively use the application to manage your items, warehouses, purchases, sales, and stock movements.

Tip: In Business Process, documents are often created in a "Draft" status and require "Submission" to finalize their impact on the system. Always ensure documents are submitted to reflect changes.

Inventory Masters

Master data forms the foundation of your inventory system. These documents define the core entities you will be tracking and managing.

Items

The `Item` Doctype defines all the products or materials you manage in your inventory. Each item has a unique code and name, a unit of measure, and can be grouped for better organization.

Key Fields:

  • Item Code: Unique identifier for the item.
  • Item Name: Display name of the item.
  • Unit of Measure: How the item is measured (e.g., Pcs, Kg).
  • Item Group: Category for the item (e.g., Raw Material, Finished Good).
  • Current Stock: Read-only field showing the current quantity available across all warehouses. This value is derived from the `Bin` records.

Usage: Create a new Item record for every distinct product or material you wish to track. Ensure all relevant details are filled accurately.

Warehouses

`Warehouse` records define the physical or logical locations where your inventory is stored. This allows you to track stock across different locations.

Key Fields:

  • Warehouse Name: Name of the storage location.
  • Address: Physical address or description.
  • Parent Warehouse: For hierarchical structures (e.g., Main Warehouse > Shelf A).

Usage: Define all your storage locations. This could be a physical building, a specific shelf, or even a virtual location like "In Transit."

Suppliers

`Supplier` records store information about the entities from whom you purchase items.

Key Fields:

  • Supplier Name: Name of the supplier.
  • Contact Person, Email, Phone, Address.

Usage: Create a Supplier record for each vendor you regularly buy from.

Customers

`Customer` records store information about the entities to whom you sell items.

Key Fields:

  • Customer Name: Name of the customer.
  • Contact Person, Email, Phone, Address.

Usage: Create a Customer record for each client you regularly sell to.

Units (UOM)

`Unit` (or Unit of Measure) defines the standard units used to quantify your items (e.g., "Pcs" for pieces, "Kg" for kilograms, "Ltr" for liters). Items are linked to these units.

Usage: Ensure all necessary units are defined before creating Items.

Item Groups

`Item Group` allows you to categorize your items into logical groups (e.g., "Raw Materials," "Finished Goods," "Packaging," "Consumables"). This helps in organizing and reporting.

Usage: Set up a logical hierarchy of item groups to classify your inventory effectively.

Bin (Item-Warehouse Stock)

The `Bin` Doctype is where the system tracks the precise quantity of each `Item` within each `Warehouse`. You generally won't create these manually, as they are automatically managed by the system whenever stock moves.

Key Fields:

  • Item Code: The specific item.
  • Warehouse: The specific location.
  • Actual Qty: The current physical quantity of the item in that warehouse.
  • Reserved Qty: Quantity reserved for sales orders or other commitments.
  • Projected Qty: The expected quantity (Actual + incoming - outgoing).

Usage: `Bin` records are crucial for accurate stock levels and availability checks. They are updated automatically by `Stock Entry` and `Stock Ledger Entry` records.

Commercial Documents

These documents represent your agreements and commitments to buy from suppliers or sell to customers. They precede the actual physical movement of goods.

Purchase Orders

A `Purchase Order` (PO) is a formal document sent to a supplier, indicating your intent to buy specific items at agreed-upon prices and terms. It does NOT affect inventory levels immediately.

Key Fields:

  • Supplier: The vendor you are buying from.
  • Order Date: When the PO was created.
  • Required By Date: Expected delivery date.
  • Items table: Details of items, quantities, and rates.
  • Status: Tracks the PO's progress (e.g., To Receive, Received).

Usage: Create a PO whenever you commit to purchasing items. Once submitted, it acts as a record of your outstanding purchases.

Sales Orders

A `Sales Order` (SO) is a formal document confirming a sale to a customer, detailing the items, quantities, and prices. It does NOT affect inventory levels immediately.

Key Fields:

  • Customer: The client you are selling to.
  • Order Date: When the SO was created.
  • Delivery Date: Expected delivery date.
  • Items table: Details of items, quantities, and rates.
  • Status: Tracks the SO's progress (e.g., To Deliver, Delivered).

Usage: Create an SO whenever you confirm a sale to a customer. Once submitted, it acts as a record of your outstanding sales.

Inventory Transactions

These documents record the actual physical movement of items, directly impacting your inventory levels.

Stock Entries

A `Stock Entry` is the primary document for recording all physical changes to your inventory. It is crucial for accurate stock tracking as it triggers updates to `Bin` and `Stock Ledger Entry` records.

Key Field: Entry Type determines the nature of the stock movement:

  • Material Receipt: For items coming into a warehouse (e.g., from a supplier, initial stock).
  • Material Issue: For items leaving a warehouse (e.g., to a customer, for internal consumption).
  • Stock Transfer: For moving items between two warehouses.
  • Stock Adjustment: For correcting inventory discrepancies (e.g., after a physical count).

Key Fields:

  • Posting Date/Time: When the movement occurred.
  • From Warehouse: Where stock is moving *from* (for Issue, Transfer).
  • To Warehouse: Where stock is moving *to* (for Receipt, Transfer, Adjustment).
  • Items table: Details of items and quantities involved.
  • Total Amount: The calculated total value of all items in this stock entry.
  • Reference Document Type/Name: Links to a Purchase Order or Sales Order if applicable.

Usage: Always create a Stock Entry for any physical movement of goods. Submitting a Stock Entry will update the `Bin` records and create `Stock Ledger Entries`.

Stock Ledger Entries

The `Stock Ledger Entry` is an immutable record of every single change to an item's quantity and value within a specific warehouse. These are automatically created by the system when a `Stock Entry` is submitted or cancelled, forming a complete audit trail of your inventory history.

Key Fields:

  • Item Code: The item involved in the change.
  • Warehouse: The location where the change occurred.
  • Qty Change: The quantity added (positive) or removed (negative).
  • Current Qty: The stock level in the warehouse *after* this transaction.
  • Valuation Rate: The unit cost/value at the time of the transaction.
  • Stock Entry: Link to the `Stock Entry` that caused this change.
  • Purpose: Describes the type of stock change (e.g., Material Receipt, Material Issue).

Usage: You will typically view `Stock Ledger Entries` through reports (like the Stock Ledger Report) rather than creating them directly. They are essential for auditing, tracing stock history, and calculating historical stock values.

Key Business Workflows

Understanding these workflows is essential for efficient inventory management. They illustrate how different documents interact to complete a business process.

Purchase to Material Receipt Flow

This flow describes the process from ordering goods to receiving them into your inventory.

Create Purchase Order
Purchase Order (PO)
Supplier Ships Goods
Create Stock Entry (Material Receipt)
Stock Entry (Material Receipt)
Stock Ledger Entry Created
Bin & Item Stock Updated
PO Status Updated

Detailed Steps:

  1. A `Purchase Order` is created and submitted to commit to buying items from a supplier.
  2. When the goods physically arrive, a `Stock Entry` of type `Material Receipt` is created, typically by fetching items from the relevant `Purchase Order`.
  3. Submitting the `Material Receipt` triggers the creation of `Stock Ledger Entry` records for each item. These ledger entries then update the `actual_qty` in the relevant `Bin` records, which in turn updates the `current_stock` on the `Item` Doctype. The `received_qty` on the `Purchase Order` items is also updated.
Sales Order to Material Issue Flow

This flow describes the process from confirming a sale to issuing goods from your inventory.

Create Sales Order
Sales Order (SO)
Prepare & Ship Goods
Create Stock Entry (Material Issue)
Stock Entry (Material Issue)
Stock Ledger Entry Created
Bin & Item Stock Updated
SO Status Updated

Detailed Steps:

  1. A `Sales Order` is created and submitted to confirm a sale to a customer.
  2. When the goods are prepared for shipment, a `Stock Entry` of type `Material Issue` is created, typically by fetching items from the relevant `Sales Order`.
  3. Submitting the `Material Issue` triggers the creation of `Stock Ledger Entry` records for each item. These ledger entries then decrease the `actual_qty` in the relevant `Bin` records, which in turn updates the `current_stock` on the `Item` Doctype. The `delivered_qty` on the `Sales Order` items is also updated.
Internal Stock Management Flow (Adjustments & Transfers)

This flow covers internal movements and corrections within your inventory.

Identify Need for Adjustment/Transfer
Create Stock Entry
Stock Entry
(Type: Adjustment or Transfer)
Stock Ledger Entry Created
Bin & Item Stock Updated

Detailed Steps:

  1. **Stock Adjustment:** Used to correct inventory quantities (e.g., after a physical count, for damaged goods). A `Stock Entry` with `Entry Type` as `Stock Adjustment` is created, specifying the `To Warehouse` and the positive or negative quantity change for each item.
  2. **Stock Transfer:** Used to move items between two warehouses. A `Stock Entry` with `Entry Type` as `Stock Transfer` is created, specifying both the `From Warehouse` and `To Warehouse`, along with the items and quantities.
  3. Submitting these `Stock Entries` triggers the creation of `Stock Ledger Entry` records, which then update the `Bin` quantities and `Item` stock levels accordingly.

Reporting

The system provides various reports to give you insights into your inventory.

To access reports:

  1. Navigate to the "Reports" section in your Desk.
  2. Look for reports related to Inventory, such as:
    • **Stock Balance Report:** Shows current stock levels per item and warehouse, primarily drawing data from `Bin` records.
    • **Stock Ledger:** Provides a detailed, auditable history of all stock movements for a specific item, drawing data from `Stock Ledger Entry` records.
    • **Open Purchase Orders:** Lists all POs that are not yet fully received.
    • **Open Sales Orders:** Lists all SOs that are not yet fully delivered.
  3. Use the filters within each report to refine your data.
